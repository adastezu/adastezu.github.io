Hi, welcome to my webpage.

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at Aalto University, Finland, in the Distributed Algorithms group of Jukka Suomela.

My research studies graph computation under severe information and resource constraints. I study how locality, memory, and limited access to the input determine which graph problems can be solved efficiently, with a particular focus on distributed and streaming computation. Across these settings, my work combines algorithm design, structural graph theory, and lower-bound techniques.

My current research agenda has two closely connected themes. The first concerns the LOCAL model and its variants, particularly the robustness of locally checkable problems, the role of model assumptions, and the complexity of graph-coloring problems. The second concerns graph streams, where I design near-linear-space algorithms and prove space lower bounds for coloring and certification problems. These themes are unified by a broader question: which computational resources and structural properties are essential for efficient graph computation?

My earlier work concerned parameterized and exact graph algorithms.

I completed my PhD at IRIF, Université Paris Cité, in December 2024, under the supervision of Pierre Fraigniaud and Adi Rosén. My thesis is titled Computation with Partial Information: An Algorithmic Investigation of Graph Problems in Distributed Computing and Streaming.

Previously, I was a visiting student in the Parameterized Complexity group at The Institute of Mathematical Sciences, Chennai. I received my M.Sc. in Computer Science from Chennai Mathematical Institute in 2018 and my B.Tech. in Computer Science and Engineering from Tezpur University in 2016.

You can find my papers on the Research page and on DBLP. The best way to reach me is by email.
